**Abstract**  
This study presents the first reported case of simultaneous intracochlear schwannoma (ICS) resection and cochlear implantation (CI) via a minimally invasive approach, achieving preserved hearing and functional outcomes. A 55-year-old male with a 20-year history of progressive bilateral sensorineural hearing loss, tinnitus, and incapacitating vertigo underwent surgery for a 0.3 cm ICS in the left cochlear apical turn. The procedure involved mastoidectomy, posterior tympanotomy, and removal of the incus and tensor tympani to access the cochlea. The tumor was resected through a small apical window, followed by round window insertion of a straight CI electrode (Nucleus CI422). Postoperatively, the patient experienced complete resolution of vertigo, improved tinnitus, and 100% closed-set speech recognition with an average audiometric threshold of 23 dB (500–2000 Hz). Bimodal stimulation (CI + contralateral hearing aid) further enhanced open-set speech recognition to 100%. The technique preserved cochlear anatomy, enabling successful CI without structural damage. This case highlights the feasibility of combined tumor resection and CI in ICS management, offering a novel approach to hearing restoration while addressing tumor-related symptoms. The study underscores the importance of advanced imaging for ICS diagnosis and advances in CI technology for preserving residual hearing. This innovation represents a significant advancement in treating intralabyrinthine schwannomas, with potential implications for broader clinical applications.
