*   **Magnetic Dipoles Interact:** Now, consider two magnets. Each magnet is essentially a collection of these aligned atomic magnetic dipoles. These dipoles interact with each other.

*   **Not "Stored" Like a Spring:** Your "spring" analogy is helpful for visualization, but it's not quite right. It's more accurate to think of the system as existing within an *energy landscape*. This landscape represents the potential energy of the two magnets as a function of their separation.

As they get closer, the magnetic fields begin to align, reducing the overall energy of the system. The aligned magnetic dipoles exert forces on each other, pulling them together.
*   **Minimum Energy:**  When the magnets snap together, they reach a *lower* energy state.  This is analogous to a ball rolling down a hill – it naturally seeks the lowest point in the energy landscape. The energy difference between the high-energy (separated) state and the low-energy (attracted) state is what manifests as the motion.
*   **Analogy to Electrostatic Potential:** This is very similar to how two oppositely charged particles interact. They have a potential energy based on their separation, and when they come together, they release energy (e.g., light) as they move to a lower energy state.

This distortion creates vibrations – quantized packets of vibrational energy called phonons. These phonons propagate through the magnet, eventually dissipating as heat.
*   **No "Loss" of Energy:** It's vital to understand that the energy isn't *lost*. It's transformed into a different form (vibrational energy) within the system. The total energy of the system (magnets + environment) remains constant (conservation of energy).

```python
def format_card(suit: str, rank: str) -> str:
    """
    Return a human‑readable representation of a playing card.

    Parameters
    ----------
    suit : str
        The suit of the card (e.g., "hearts", "spades", "diamonds", "clubs").
    rank : str
        The rank of the card (e.g., "A", "2", "10", "J", "Q", "K").

    Returns
    -------
    str
        A string in the form "rank of suit", e.g. "A of spades".
    """
    # Basic validation (optional)
    if not isinstance(suit, str) or not isinstance(rank, str):
        raise TypeError("Both suit and rank must be strings.")
    if not suit or not rank:
        raise ValueError("Both suit and rank must be non‑empty strings.")

    # Normalise the strings (trim whitespace, lower‑case the suit if desired)
    suit_clean = suit.strip()
    rank_clean = rank.strip()

    return f"{rank_clean} of {suit_clean}"


# Example usage
if __name__ == "__main__":
    print(format_card("hearts", "A"))      # → A of hearts
    print(format_card("spades", "10"))    # → 10 of spades
    print(format_card("clubs", "K"))      # → K of clubs
```

### How it works
1. **Parameter checking** – The function first ensures both inputs are strings and not empty. This prevents accidental misuse (e.g., passing `None` or an integer).
2. **Cleaning** – `strip()` removes any leading/trailing whitespace the caller might have included.
3. **Formatting** – An f‑string (`f"{rank_clean} of {suit_clean}"`) builds the final output in the requested `"rank of suit"` format.

You can easily adapt this to other languages (JavaScript, Java, C#, etc.)—the core idea is the same: concatenate the rank, the literal `" of "`, and the suit.
